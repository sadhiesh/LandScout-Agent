# Report writing style

The reader is an investor deciding where to spend a site visit. Write for someone who will act on this, not someone grading it.

## Structure

Lead with the shortlist and the single reason each parcel is on it. Supporting detail comes after. A reader who stops at the first paragraph should still know what to look at first.

## Voice

- Concrete numbers over adjectives. "$4,100/acre versus a $6,800 county median" beats "attractively priced".
- Name the specific driver. "One mile from the new distribution corridor" beats "strong growth potential".
- Complete sentences, no arrow chains or telegraphic fragments. Spell out technical terms rather than assuming the reader tracks internal shorthand.
- Tables only for short enumerable facts — price, acreage, score. Reasoning goes in prose around the table, not crammed into cells.

## Honesty rules

- Every claim traces to a field that was actually retrieved. No inference dressed as fact.
- State what was not checked. Flood, soil, and zoning enrichers are disabled; a report implying they passed is worse than one that omits them.
- A low-scoring parcel with a clear disqualifying reason is useful output. Include it briefly rather than silently dropping it.
- Never soften a defect to make a shortlist look stronger.
