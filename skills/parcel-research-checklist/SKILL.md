# Parcel research checklist

Work in this order. Each step feeds the next, and stopping early is fine as long as you say where you stopped.

1. **Identify** — resolve the parcel to a stable id (LandWatch `property_id`) and its county and state. Everything downstream keys off this.
2. **Listing facts** — price, acreage, price per acre, days on market, price history. Price history matters more than list price; two cuts in 90 days tells you more than the asking number.
3. **Location context** — county, nearest city, and how the parcel sits relative to it. A cheap parcel two hours from anything is cheap for a reason.
4. **Comparables** — price per acre against the county median for a similar acreage band. Always state the sample this median came from; a median over six listings is not a market.
5. **Physical characteristics** — terrain, water, and access from the detail description and amenities. Note explicitly when access is unstated, which is common and material.
6. **Utilities** — power, water, septic. Absence is frequently unstated rather than stated as absent. Treat silence as unknown, not as "no".
7. **Constraints not yet automated** — flood, soil, and zoning enrichers ship disabled. Say so rather than implying these were checked.
8. **Summarize** — what makes this parcel interesting, what would kill the deal, and what you could not verify.

## The rule

Step 8 must distinguish three states: verified, unavailable, and not checked. Collapsing "unavailable" into "fine" is how a bad parcel reaches a shortlist.
